Postdoc — Deep learning for functional-oxide growth (OXYD-IA), École Centrale de Lyon

Écully (Lyon) — INL & LIRIS  ·  12 months, start ~January 2027  ·  2 800 € gross / month   Deadline 30 Oct 2026

The OXYD-IA project aims at predicting the final XRD spectrum of an oxide thin film directly from the RHEED video recorded during its MBE growth, opening the way to predictive in situ control of oxide epitaxy. The work couples a spatio-temporal video encoder with probabilistic regression and calibrated uncertainty, trained on a unique unpublished dataset of paired RHEED videos and XRD spectra. Candidates need a PhD in AI (computer vision, machine learning or signal processing) with 2–5 years of post-PhD experience, hands-on practice with 3D CNNs, temporal transformers or probabilistic models in PyTorch, and a genuine appetite for experimental physics. Supervision is shared between Clarisse Furgeaud (INL) and Stéphane Derrode (LIRIS), with access to the Équipex NANOFUTUR MBE line and GPU resources.

Apply on the École Centrale de Lyon portal

Postdoc (18 months) — Machine learning & molecular simulation of ion-induced pressure in porous materials (MINT), Montpellier

Montpellier — LMGC / L2C, Université de Montpellier  ·  18 months, start 2026   Rolling review

The MINT project asks how ions confined at the nanoscale can generate mechanical pressures large enough to contribute to the failure of porous materials, using the alkali-silica reaction in cement as a model system. The postdoc will lead the computational component: developing and training machine-learning interatomic potentials for silicate gel / water / ion interfaces, running large-scale molecular dynamics and enhanced-sampling simulations such as metadynamics, and quantifying the molecular origins of disjoining and ion-correlation pressures. The project brings together Romain Dupuis, Simona Ispas and Thibault Sohier alongside experimentalists, with access to the national HPC centres (IDRIS, TGCC, CINES). Experience with MACE, DeepMD, MTP or ab initio MD is particularly valuable. Applications — CV, short research statement and 2–3 referees — are reviewed upon receipt.

Contact : romain.dupuis@umontpellier.fr

 

Postdoc (3 years) — ML-driven phase transitions in nanoparticles, Lille

Villeneuve d'Ascq — UMET, Université de Lille  ·  3 years

Temperature-driven phase transitions in nanoparticles, tracked by atomistic simulation: the project first develops and benchmarks machine-learning interaction potentials of increasing complexity for metallic alloys and oxides, then combines brute-force and rare-event sampling to isolate freezing and melting nucleation pathways, with results confronted to experiments from collaborators in the same consortium. The offer stresses a small team with direct PI mentorship, the chance to supervise students, and support in preparing a CNRS permanent-researcher application. Applications consist of a CV including two references and a cover letter detailing the candidate's interest; no closing date is given.

Full description · Contact : julien.lam@cnrs.fr

 

Postdoc (18 months) — Machine learning for d/f-metal catalysis (MAD-CAT), Toulouse

Toulouse — LPCNO, Université de Toulouse  ·  18 months, NanoX-funded  ·  ≈2 500–2 800 € net / month   Rolling until filled

MAD-CAT turns two decades of accumulated DFT data on d- and f-block metal complexes — hundreds of transition states and reaction intermediates — into a curated, open machine-learning dataset, then uses it to fine-tune a MACE equivariant potential coupled to the AFIR method for automated reaction-network discovery. Two application axes: CO2 reduction and stereoselective polymerization, with catalytic depolymerization of plastics as the longer-term goal. Priority goes to candidates with expertise in MACE (or other ML interatomic potentials) and/or AFIR, together with a solid background in DFT and reaction mechanisms. The team holds ≈2 million CPU/GPU hours per year at CALMIP. Applications — CV, publication list, short research statement, two referees — are reviewed on a rolling basis.

Contact : laurent.maron@utoulouse.fr · romuald.poteau@utoulouse.fr

Publications marquantes  / Notable papers
 

RADAR-PD — automated multiphase identification across X-ray and neutron powder diffraction

Yadav, Cheng & Doucet (Oak Ridge National Laboratory)  ·  APL Machine Learning, 1 Sept 2026   Peer-reviewed

A mismatch-tolerant network works on coarse momentum-transfer fingerprints, then hands its hypotheses to automated lattice nudging and physics-constrained Rietveld verification: dominant phases are proposed from elemental constraints, secondary phases stripped out recursively. On the experimental RRUFF powder XRD benchmark it beats data-driven automated Rietveld analysis (DARA) at recovering the reference phase. The part that stands out is the neutron coverage — time-of-flight and constant-wavelength datasets, where comparable automated tools have been essentially absent — which makes it instrument-agnostic in a way single-modality XRD classifiers are not.

doi:10.1063/5.0345499

 

Atomic electron tomography from sparse projections, with a diffusion model as the prior

Li, Cui, Su, Lei, Yu, Yang, Li & Gan  ·  Nature Communications, 16 June 2026   Peer-reviewed · open access

Atomic electron tomography resolves 3D structure in real space but normally demands many projections over a wide tilt range, at a dose and acquisition time that rule out most samples. NSIRE uses an unsupervised diffusion model to iteratively compute null-space solutions of the projection equations, so reconstructions satisfy the measured projections while being pulled onto the manifold of plausible atomic potentials. It resolves complex materials from 6–12° sparse projections, or within a ±29° tilt range, without retraining — demonstrated on a 4 nm Pt nanoparticle containing grain boundaries and a 3 nm PtCo nanoalloy, the smallest resolved so far, at sub-20 pm RMS displacement.

doi:10.1038/s41467-026-74189-4

 

EXAFS as a referee for machine-learning potentials: local lattice dynamics of hcp zinc

Dimitrijevs, Žguns, Pudza, Kalinko & Kuzmin (Riga)  ·  Physica B 739, 418945 (October 2026)   Peer-reviewed · free preprint

Temperature-dependent X-ray absorption spectroscopy gives atomic displacements out to eight coordination shells in hcp zinc, resolving the strong anisotropy between in-plane and out-of-plane motion. Used as a test of machine-learning potentials, it is unforgiving: the original potential overestimates thermal disorder, and a refined version is needed before simulation and measurement agree. A useful reminder that a spectroscopic observable sensitive to local dynamics discriminates between potentials that energy and force benchmarks rate as equivalent.

doi:10.1016/j.physb.2026.418945 · free full text (arXiv)

 

An agentic AI X-ray scientist — an LLM agent that ran a real beamline experiment

Chen, Petsch, Israelski, Plumley, Shen et al. (SLAC, MIT, Northeastern)  ·  Nature Machine Intelligence, July 2026   Peer-reviewed · open access

An agent built on existing large language models, extended through the Model Context Protocol, that plans experimental tasks, executes them, analyses what comes back and iterates toward a stated scientific goal. It was trained against an in-house virtual six-circle diffractometer, then transferred to a real synchrotron beamline, where it identified reference reflections and determined the orientation matrix — the unavoidable first step of any single-crystal scattering experiment — and adapted when conditions departed from what it expected. The claim is deliberately narrow and all the more interesting for it: not a self-driving laboratory, but an agent that handled the alignment work a beamline scientist does by hand, on real hardware.

doi:10.1038/s42256-026-01261-5
