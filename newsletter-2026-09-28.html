Publications récentes  / Recent papers
 

Unsupervised deep learning for limited-angle STEM-EDX tomography

Del Pozo Bueno, Brosset, Monniez, Navarro, Ciuciu & Saghi (CEA-Leti, Inria, Université Grenoble Alpes)  ·  npj Computational Materials, 18 Sept 2026   Characterization · peer-reviewed · open access

Energy-dispersive X-ray tomography in STEM gives three-dimensional elemental mapping at the nanoscale, but a restricted tilt range produces missing-wedge artefacts and the electron dose must be limited to prevent beam damage. The authors introduce an unsupervised framework combining a Deep Image Prior with total-variation regularization, and a multi-channel formulation, DIPm-TV, that reconstructs several elemental volumes jointly by exploiting their spatial correlations. Applied to two Ge–Sb–Te memory devices at ±40° tilt and a dose of 2×105 e–/Å2, it yields voxel-by-voxel elemental maps with near-isotropic resolution from the EDX signals alone, without external structural priors such as HAADF-STEM images.

doi:10.1038/s41524-026-02325-1

 

Field-aware, charge-informed machine learning for response properties and vibrational spectra

Chen & Luber (University of Zurich)  ·  npj Computational Materials, 4 Sept 2026   Simulation · peer-reviewed · open access

Accurately capturing the effect of external electric fields remains a challenge for machine-learning interatomic potentials. After showing the limitations of conventional architectures on field-dependent response properties, the authors introduce a physics-informed, field-aware equivariant neural network in which the field is incorporated through an equivariant embedding layer, response properties are obtained from an energy-derivative approach within a single architecture, and a charge-equilibration electrostatic layer captures long-range interactions; dipoles computed from the charges and from the energy derivative agree closely. The framework is used to predict response properties and to simulate IR and Raman spectra across molecular and periodic systems at different external field strengths.

doi:10.1038/s41524-026-02293-6

 

ChatXRD — an LLM-driven framework for crystal-system classification and lattice-parameter prediction from XRD

He, Cao, Chen, Hu, Xu, Xiao & Chen (USTC & Suzhou Research Institute)  ·  Materials Genome Engineering Advances, 7 Sept 2026   Characterization · peer-reviewed · open access

ChatXRD integrates a GPT-5-driven agent with two custom-designed tools, CrystalSystemClassifier and LatticeParametersPredictor, both based on a self-attention-decayed transformer model tailored for XRD data, and uses decision-tree-based reasoning to carry out the task. The reported performance is above 97 % accuracy for crystal-system classification and 0.88 to 0.99 for lattice-parameter prediction, which the authors give as surpassing current state-of-the-art approaches.

doi:10.1002/mgea.70095

 

Structural benchmarks overlook density-of-states errors in machine-learned interatomic potentials

Tseng, Han, Li, Xu, Hu, Qiu & Oses (Johns Hopkins University)  ·  APL Machine Learning, 1 Sept 2026   Simulation · peer-reviewed · open access

Standard benchmarks for universal machine-learned interatomic potentials emphasise energies, forces and relaxed geometries, leaving unexplored whether the resulting structures also preserve the density of states. Relaxing oxides, iodides and alloys with four such potentials, recomputing the density of states with a common density-functional protocol and comparing against full-DFT references, the authors find that structural benchmark performance does not predict electronic agreement in any of the three material classes. They report that the hybrid workflow remains useful: single-point DFT calculations on machine-learned geometries recover Fermi energies, reduce convergence cost and approximate the ensemble-averaged density of states of a disordered high-entropy alloy from a small subset of ordered representatives.

doi:10.1063/5.0339982
